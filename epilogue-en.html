Epilogue

A damp train station, warm summer air, and that very same feeling of anticipation I had unsuccessfully tried to suppress for the past two days.

— Well, if you want, you can leave this beer for them to try. — Artyom said, holding out a glass bottle of beer.

— Nah, they can do without it. I think I'll drink it myself on the train. And there it is. But next time, you're coming with me.

We flicked our cigarette butts away, I got on the train, and immediately dialed the number.

— Oh, it's Andrey, hi! — a voice came through.

— Andrey, hi, it's Yana. — I heard another voice.

— Hi, sweetheart, it's Miroslav! — I had been warned about him. So I decided to play along a little.

— Hi, my little cutie. — I said softly, and then, calmly, almost monotonously. — I'm already on the train. I'll be there in two hours.

— Alright, we're waiting. Text or call when you arrive. — I heard that first voice again, and then I said:

— Alright, El.

I hung up and put on "Take a Slice" in my headphones to calm my anxiety and mentally prepare myself. On the train, I listened to music, watched "The Boys", and twenty minutes before arriving, I went to the bathroom and opened that very bottle of beer. When I finished it, I took some nicotine and waited for the arrival.

I arrived five minutes late and, as soon as I got off the train, took a breath of the capital's air. But before I could even reach the exit, I was shot with a water gun. It was Ela. I shook her hand in greeting and asked:

— Where are the others? — I didn't even finish the question before I saw Yana, two random guys, and some girl.

I held out my hand to the first person next to me. He introduced himself as Sasha, the other was Miroslav, the girl turned out to be Sofia, and the last one you already know — Yana. We went onto the escalator while talking along the way. My voice was low, and my gaze was more blurred. On the way to the exit, Miron took Ela by the hand, which, to put it mildly, I did not expect. We left the station, and Yana immediately offered me a cigarette, while Sasha and Miron opened some cider. We shared the cider with the guys, and I pointed Ela toward that very hair tie she had given me.

— As you can see, I dug it out, haha.

— Oh, actually, yeah.

Sasha left to catch his train, and the five of us went to the riverside.

— What kind of movies or TV shows do you watch? — I asked Miron.

— Well, I'm not really a movie person. I mostly watch TV shows...

He named the basic teenage starter pack: The Boys, Breaking Bad, You, and others.

— Predictable. — I said briefly, with a slight hint of irony.

The guy seemed normal until he started putting on a show. I never understood what the reason was, but at some point Ela and Miron started chasing each other around. Meanwhile, I watched cold-bloodedly. But less than five minutes later, the heaviest downpour began. They all held hands and decided to run to the library. I didn't care about the rain. I simply walked confidently behind them.

We entered the library, the girls went somewhere, and Miron and I were left alone.

— By the way, what do you know about me? — I started the conversation without any introduction.

— Well, I was told that you're kind, you've known Diana for a long time, you go to the gym, and that's about it.

— Wait, who? Oh, Diana... — I replied with a smirk.

— Yeah. What do you do with your life anyway?

— Eh... I go to the gym, mess around, and I also just finished writing a story.

— Oh wow, what kind?

— I'll just send you a link to the website with the story, okay?

— Yeah, sure.

We exchanged Telegram usernames, although I already knew his. Before the meeting, I had briefly looked through all of these people on Telegram. So none of it was a surprise to me. When the girls returned, we went to one of the floors of the library and sat down there. I stayed silent, staring out the window, scrolling through TikTok, and then left to call Artyom.

— What's up, dude. Basically, I feel like some kind of outcast. Everything seems fine, but something feels off. They're talking about their own personal topics, things I know nothing about.

— Try to blend in somehow. Start a conversation with someone, just about something random.

— You think it'll work?

— I think so.

I returned to my spot and decided to give it a try. And it worked. I started a conversation with Sonya about something mundane, and then it turned into a philosophical discussion about life and death. We went outside and continued talking. Miron joined us too. It also surprised me that they didn't know the name "Ela". They only knew her as Diana. As it turned out, Ela was Diana. While we were standing there talking, three complete strangers approached us. One was with a girl, the other wasn't. They immediately started talking to Miron. They were probably his friends. When the conversation turned to who he was there with, he immediately started stumbling.

— Ooooh... You're hanging out with a girl? Can we see her? — someone from the trio said.

— Well, yeah, with a girl, but no, I'm not showing her.

— Come on... Show us.

— Nah, I don't want to. Heh.

— Eh, whatever you want.

— Alright then, I'll bring her over to you, okay?

— Deal.

We went back into the library, went up to the floor where the girls were sitting, and Miron went to get Ela while we stayed standing. Miron brought Ela and Yana over. They said hello and almost immediately left. After they were gone, Sonya received a text from Ela: "We went outside." Miron became awkward because Ela had texted Sonya rather than him.

— So? What do you think of her?

— Is that the smaller one? — the guy's girlfriend looked at Miron with more pity than support.

— Yeah.

The girl looked at him again, as if choosing her words carefully so as not to hurt him.

— Well... I don't know. She seemed kind of scared...

— You're not judging, right? Everything's okay?

At that point I couldn't take it anymore. I sat down next to him and said:

— Why the fuck do you even care about someone else's approval? Especially in this situation? — I said it so coldly that there was even a hint of cynicism in my voice. But Miron didn't react.

— Well, let her be okay... — the girl finally managed to say.

After that, we went downstairs to catch up with the girls. I decided not to take the escalator with the others and chose the elevator instead. The doors opened, and I saw two familiar faces.

— Ela and Yana, huh. — a sinister smile involuntarily crept across my face.

— What did they say? — Yana snorted, as if she already knew the answer.

— What, you think I'm going to tell you everything?

— Well, they don't know that you can tell us.

— In short — they're not very happy.

— Yeah, whatever. Anyway, we'll leave this place and get away from them.

— I'm coming with you? — I asked.

— Up to you. — Yana answered coldly.

I followed them. We left the busy library and headed toward the station, passing by them. But before we could get very far, Miron ran up to us.

— Oh, you're here... Alright, I'll come over later. — and at that very moment, he ran away from us.

Before the station, we smoked. But at some point, Ela turned to me and held out an opened but unused pack of Prince Red cigarettes:

— Haha, what's with the generosity? Thanks.

— It's new, just opened.

Already at the station, we sat down on the steps. I asked something ordinary, like "How are you?", and they predictably answered "Not great." I decided not to drag the conversation out and simply scrolled through TikTok beside Yana.

Miron came running toward us, out of breath, saying:

— They're coming. Run!

Yana finally couldn't take it anymore and answered him:

— Why the fuck do we need to run? Calm down.

But he didn't care. We moved somewhere else. Miron came over again and started clinging to Ela. And since she wasn't in the mood, she didn't answer him. The whole scene only made me feel absurdity and nothing more. When everyone got bored, we went outside. We reached some church where there was nobody around. We sat on the steps, opened some cider, and Miron immediately drank half the can in one gulp, giving the other half to me.

Everything was fine until he started talking about his pet — a cat. He suggested that Ela come to his place and see it. Ela was stunned, and even I turned toward him with the most cynical expression possible and said:

— What the fuck?

Ela didn't hesitate either and replied:

— Well, I'm not leaving Yana and Andrey. If you pay for the ticket, then okay.

Miron got a little flustered, apparently calculating how much it would cost him. In the end, he backed down, saying "next time."

But less than ten minutes later, he did something else. This time, when the girls were talking about a blade, this genius blurted something out that finally made even Yana angry:

— What did you say?

— No, nothing... — Miron backed down.

— No, now even I'm curious what kind of bullshit you just said, haha. — I joined the conversation too, but he didn't answer anymore.

— He said something about the blade. — Yana answered me.

— Are you fucking stupid? Haha. — I said it coldly, with mockery in my voice. But Miron didn't open his mouth again.

Everyone quickly stopped paying attention to Miron and decided to return to the station. On the way, I almost laughed out loud at how desperately Miron was trying to get closer to Ela, while she didn't want that at the time.

— Haha, Artyom is definitely missing here! — I said with a slight smirk.

— He would've fucking roasted me? — Miron replied.

— You got that right.

Miron apparently already understood that he was doing something stupid, but did nothing about it. When we arrived, we sat down on a damp step. Still, I decided to talk to him privately.

— Miron, a word.

— Yeah, sure.

We stepped behind a wall that already reeked of his tension.

— Listen, I'll be honest with you. You're acting like an annoying fucking idiot. Stop bothering her. If she doesn't want it, then don't put on a fucking show. And honestly, I see myself from a year ago in you. But if you really want everything to be okay, stop doing all this. It's in your own interest. Got it? Now act dumb and let's talk about where we should go instead. — I wasn't coddling him. I spoke directly, looking him in the eyes. It felt like I could burn a hole through him with my stare.

— Yeah, I got it. Thanks.

As soon as we returned to the girls, Yana immediately took him aside for "a few words" as well. Ela and I were left alone.

— I didn't expect us to meet here. It seemed impossible. — I said.

— Yeah.

— So much has happened in a year... How are things with you?

— Same as always. You already know everything.

— True. Let's take a photo, for the memories?

— No, I...

— Just cover your face with your hands. — I interrupted her.

— Okay.

She moved closer to me, and we took a photo.

— Do you mind if I post it on my Telegram channel?

— Yeah, just blur my face, please.

— Sure.

— Is "collab of the year" written with one "l"?

— Yeah, I think so. Hahaha.

I posted the photo with the caption "Collab of the year. Right, El?" She was still sitting next to me, and I was enjoying it. But our peace was interrupted by Miron. He flew toward her like a bullet, sat down on the other side of her, and sloppily put an arm around her. When Yana returned, we went back to the entrance of the library. We smoked again, talked about my previous trip to Ela's city, and once again I decided to have a talk with Miron.

— I want to give you a polite warning. She can hurt herself and she can hurt you. She's like a cat that plays and scratches without even realizing it. But either way, if you hurt her, you'll be talking to me. Understood?

— Yeah, I got it. About the "cat" thing — yeah, I've thought about that.

We returned to the girls, who were already discussing the way back. We gathered our things and went to the station. There we found Ela and Yana's platform and sat on a bench. I was about to go find my own platform when I noticed that mine was literally right behind me. An amazing coincidence. But the difference was that our trains only crossed paths here. After this stop, they would go in completely different directions.

Throughout the entire walk, Yana seemed to stare right through me. But when I said that I was already leaving, she was the first to stand up and hold out her hand. Miron stood up next. We hugged. Ela didn't even get up. She simply held out her hand. After that, I went onto the train.

Looking at it all — Ela lying with her head on Miron's lap while Yana sat beside them — I understood everything clearly now. Miron was just like me, a year ago. But here's a spoiler for you — nothing happened between them. Anyway, after the walk, Ela told me that Yana was even afraid of me. Yeah, maybe I acted too cynical at times, but I don't give a shit.

The meeting turned out to be interesting. But it was even more interesting around the time of my graduation. I took photos with my classmates, but the day before graduation I went out with Masha's group. Yeah, the one who first showed interest and then ignored me and blocked me.

— Hey guys, like my latest TikTok video. — Masha said. I tried to find her, but since she had blocked me back in the autumn, even her account wouldn't show up for me.

When I told her I couldn't find her, I handed her my phone so she could find herself. A minute later, she finally unblocked me and liked all her own posts from my phone. After that wonderful walk, we completely forgot about each other.

A few days later, I went to Poland. Yeah, just like a year before. The difference was that this time I was simply relaxing and finally dared to go on some of the most extreme rides. It was actually amazing. For a moment, I even forgot about all the events I'd been through during the year. While in Poland, I also met an old friend whom I hadn't seen for a year, during the time of my "tragedy". I briefly told her about everything that had happened that year, and she was a little surprised.

After returning to Norway, Artyom, Misha, Sasha (Misha's sister, and the one who used to be Yulia's friend), another girl whom Artyom had brought along, and I decided to relax a little and drink something stronger than coffee. While drunk, I was briefly overcome by nostalgia for the warmth I had once received. Thankfully, even then I understood that warmth wasn't worth the sleepless nights, nerves, and time. Sasha felt the same. She told me that the entire Felles community had seen me trying to hit on Yulia, but they had also all seen that she had friend-zoned me.

— And besides, we dated...

— What, really?

— Yeah.

— She used you, that's why she wouldn't let you go.

— I don't deny it.

A couple of days later, while I was on my way to meet Zlata, I ran into Yegor. I didn't feel that way about him anymore. We talked calmly and I went on my way. When I met Zlata, who happens to be Yulia's friend, we discussed a few things, including Yulia. Zlata showed me part of their conversation, and I saw that right after her lies were exposed and after the breakup, Yulia had written to Zlata to fish for information about what I thought about everything. I just found her actions funny.

During one of my walks with Artyom, I asked him:

— So, if you were drunk, would you fuck a girl who was okay with it and who was also drunk?

— No, that's kind of immoral.

— Bro, we've done so many immoral things that this is just nothing.

— No, still, I don't think she'd enjoy it, because she's drunk and all that.

— Haha, well, you decide for yourself, man.

Then, the next day, I asked Pushisty the same question, and he answered:

— In principle, yeah. What's wrong with that?

But something happened inside me at some point... I involuntarily remembered a video that would have described me and Pushisty perfectly.

“Everyone who loves you dies inside because of you. Now Pushisty. The last person on Earth who saw something good in you, and you... turned him into yourself.”

Simply because I understood that he hadn't always been like this. I can give countless examples of situations where his conscience would have eaten him alive. But now — not anymore. I myself had become different. Completely different. I don't care about other people's opinions, their feelings, and so on. But I used to be just like Pushisty. Even Artyom now seems more moral and sensitive than I am... Maybe that's for the better, but that realization stayed with me for a long time.

Less than three months later, Dima arrived. I found out about it completely by accident. During a walk with Kira, Julia, Artyom and Nina (I spent New Year's Eve with the first three), Kira met Dima through a mutual group and they planned to meet, but that wasn't happening. I was categorically against it. I wanted to finish what I had started — beat the shit out of him. But many people disagreed, because technically there was no reason for it. All of that had happened almost six months ago, and we shouldn't forget who started it in the first place.

— So you fight him, and then what? You'll feel good only in the moment, and afterwards your conscience will eat you alive. And that's not even mentioning the possible consequences of the fight. You could accidentally hit him wrong and he could end up disabled for life, or even die. What are you going to feel after that? — Julia said to me.

— Nah, I won't give a shit about what happens after the fight. — I answered her.

— And what consequences will it have for you? What if he comes with a knife, or after the fight he reports you to the police?

— Nah, both of those are unlikely.

— Fine, then what's the reason for this overwhelming desire?

— I've already told you... From the very beginning, when I told Dima that I had fallen in love with Redhead Yulia, he said he wasn't interested in her. But a couple of months later, I found out that he was actually flirting with her quite a lot. Yeah, I remember him saying that "it was all just jokes" and so on, but I didn't believe him. Yeah, I remember Yulia allowed him to write things like that and that it was partly her fault, but the fact remains that I was the first to tell him about my feelings for her, and without a second thought he started showering her with the kind of flawless attention that Yulia wanted and that I was supposed to give her too... — at that moment, something suddenly clicked. But I had always known it. I just hadn't realized it...

— She plays with people like they're little figurines... But if everything is so bad, why fight over her at all?

— I'm not fighting over her. I want him to know that I'm not all talk. I want you guys to see that I'm not some weakling who can't fight back. I want to prove it, at least to myself.

— I don't know how else to explain to you that fighting isn't the answer. — Julia said, but by that point I was already lost in thought.

About ten minutes later, I decided to surrender. I stepped aside and called Dima. I told him that I had been wrong, that I had started blaming him without understanding the situation, and I apologized. After that conversation, I felt like shit. During the call, Dima looked at me and spoke condescendingly, as if he was right. But admitting that was something I couldn't do.

Remember Yegor? The guy who acted like a hypocritical and arrogant bastard? Well, he showed up again, and he wasn't the only one. Somewhere near the end of July, before the Felles camp (which I wanted to attend), we ran into each other by chance. By then, my anger had already passed, so I talked to him calmly. But that didn't last. I wasn't accepted into the camp, and through information from Pushisty, I realized that Yegor was talking about me behind my back in a bad way. On top of that, since he was one of the organizers, he asked for me to be blacklisted and never allowed to attend those events. I was shocked by the news, and that desire I'd had since February came rushing back — to beat the shit out of him.

During the days when I wanted to meet him on the street and finish what I had started, a well-known person from the fourth season appeared. And I'm talking about Yulia. At the beginning of August, she sent a mountain of voice messages to Kira, my friend whom I constantly hung out with. In them, Yulia said that a friend of hers was sending her my posts from my Telegram channel, and that she wanted to know what was going on between me and Yegor (because a few days earlier I had made a dramatic post about him). She also said that she didn't care, but didn't want me to have problems in the city where Felles was located. She also said that because of her, I had problems with Leo, but as we all realized, it was already August and Leo was nowhere to be seen or heard. The last time he wrote was only after he saw my photo with Ela on my Telegram channel. He threatened again that he would come. But once again — he didn't.

Back to Yegor. Before finishing what I had started, I talked to my friends again. With Julia and Artyom. Unlike Artyom, who simply supported my idea of dealing with him, Julia once again knocked some sense into me. She explained that people like him can't be fixed with a fist. If I beat the shit out of him, nothing good would come of it. At the very least, he'd keep talking behind my back, and at worst, he could report me to the police, although that was unlikely. Yeah, half my city doesn't like him, but in the city where Felles is located, he's actually respected. So if I beat him up, he'd make it look like I attacked him for no reason. And I didn't need that. But I know that the entire Felles community already knows about our situation with Yegor. I decided to back down again. I only wrote to him that I wasn't going to do anything to him because I knew life would beat him up enough on its own. So what do we have in the end? That's right — Yegor is still an asshole, Yulia wanted attention again, so she wrote to Kira because she knew the information would eventually reach me.

A couple of days later, while walking with Julia and Nina, we ran into Yegor. He immediately started the conversation:

— Forgive me... I just don't understand, I didn't say anything about you.

We talked and parted on peaceful terms.

Also, in the following days, Yulia blocked almost everyone who was talking to me. And, basically, she blocked me too. I'm actually glad about that. But it's not impossible that Redhead will appear again someday. Still, let that remain nothing more than an empty thought rather than reality.

I spent this summer with Artyom, Nina, Kira, and Julia. We had sleepovers where we messed around until morning, then drank tea and ate pizza. Shooting stars, a failed eclipse, running away from home, getting drunk, and much more. You can't tell the whole story in a single epilogue. Because that's already a story for another time...

And for now, I can say without hesitation...

Happy End.
